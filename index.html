About

My research examines how information moves through today's fragmented digital platforms understood as complex systems, and how news organizations and journalists connect communities that would otherwise have little contact with one another. My work brings together journalism, network science, and artificial intelligence. Much of my recent research develops the idea that journalism functions as a form of brokerage, carrying knowledge across the structural gaps between disconnected publics, and I use network analysis and machine learning to study that process at scale, from the news culture of Elizabethan England to contemporary social media.

Social Media and Digital Politics: Networked Reason in an Age of Digital Emotion (Routledge, 2025), written with Jeffrey Layne Blevins, examines how the architecture of social media platforms shapes political discourse. In 2026 it received the Tankard Book Award from the Association for Education in Journalism and Mass Communication, which recognizes the most outstanding book in the field of journalism and communication. My next book, Shakespeare in the News (Routledge, forthcoming 2026), uses network science to offer a new history of journalism, tracing how news organizations from the reign of Elizabeth I to the era of digital platforms have drawn on a shared cultural reference point to make political events intelligible to their readers.

I have applied these methods across a number of fields, including journalism and social media studies, information science, biomedical informatics, computational law, environmental history, and the digital humanities, where my career began. My articles have appeared in the Harvard Data Science Review, New Media and Society, Applied Linguistics, the Journal of Hospital Medicine, PMLA, and the Stanford Journal of Law, Economics, and Business, as well as in the proceedings of the AI for Libraries, Archives, and Museums conference, published by the British Library and the Library of Congress. As part of my commitment to interdisciplinary data science, I serve as an associate editor of the Harvard Data Science Review.

As Director of Research at Medill's Knight Lab and Associate University Librarian for Academic Innovation at Northwestern, I also direct projects that make large archives available to researchers and journalists. These include a public research platform for the largest archive of United States environmental impact statements and the Model of Models (MoM), an ensemble AI platform that presents complex models as explorable networks. This work has been supported by more than $5 million in funding from the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation, the Institute of Museum and Library Services, and the National Science Foundation.

Research Projects

Model of Models (MoM)

An ensemble AI research platform that runs multiple modeling strategies (LDA, word2vec, transformer embeddings) and displays complex AI models as network visualizations for pattern recognition across disciplines.

Carbon Footprint Historical Map

An interactive historical map of carbon emissions, part of the broader Anthropocene research program examining how cultural archives encode evidence of climate change.

History Moves Archive

A digital archive and public exhibition platform developed through the Mellon Catalyst Award, supporting community-built archival collections.

Archival Stratigraphy

A book-length project under contract with MIT Press investigating cultural archives as climate sensors. Applies NLP and AI methods to recover latent knowledge about the Anthropocene embedded in textual and film archives.

IMLS Generative AI Library Search

“Shaping the Conversation: Enriching Collection Access and Use with Generative AI,” the project that produced the first generative AI library search for academic collections at Northwestern.

Environmental Impact Statements Archive

A two-year, $500,000 Mellon Foundation project to digitize environmental impact statements and make them searchable.

Speaker bio

James Jaehoon Lee is an associate professor at Northwestern University’s Medill School of Journalism, director of research at Knight Lab, and associate university librarian for academic innovation. He combines network science and language modeling to build and analyze networks of ideas, topics, and people across disciplines and historical periods. His book Social Media and Digital Politics, coauthored with Jeffrey Layne Blevins, won the 2026 Tankard Book Award. He is an associate editor of the Harvard Data Science Review.
